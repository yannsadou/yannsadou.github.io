Cryogenic characterization & module integration for the BICEP Array instrument at the South Pole

As a Research Assistant at Caltech under Prof. James Bock, I contributed to the end-to-end readiness of BICEP Array optical modules for deployment and operations at the South Pole. My work focused on cryogenic characterization (optical efficiency, electrical checks, FTS measurements, SQUID tuning), micro-wirebonding of module interconnects in collaboration with NASA JPL, and mechanical design for a Caltech-inspired CMB-S4 prototype wafer implementation.

BICEP Array — South Pole CMB instrument
Context

What is BICEP Array?

BICEP Array is a next-generation ground-based CMB polarization experiment operating at the South Pole, designed to push sensitivity in the search for primordial B-modes and to precisely characterize polarized foregrounds. The project combines advanced detector arrays, superconducting readout (SQUIDs), and cryogenic receiver technology, coordinated through a high-profile multi-institution consortium.

  • Harsh environment constraints: reliability, integration discipline, and deployability
  • Module-level characterization: electrical integrity, optical throughput, spectral response
  • Readout readiness: SQUID tuning and stable operating points
My role

From lab characterization to module readiness

My contributions sat at the interface of detector physics, cryogenic test operations, and hands-on assembly: I helped ensure that optical modules were electrically robust, optically validated, and mechanically integration-ready, with particular attention to clean measurement practices and repeatable procedures.

  • OE (optical efficiency) and spectral verification with FTS
  • Electrical checks, continuity screening, and troubleshooting
  • SQUID tuning and readout stability checks
  • Micro-wirebonding of module interconnects (with NASA JPL collaboration)
  • Mechanical design responsibility on a Caltech-inspired CMB-S4 prototype wafer implementation
Workstreams

How readiness is built in a long-running research instrument

BICEP Array is not a short engineering program with fixed “milestones”. Instead, progress is driven by recurring test campaigns, integration gates, and cross-institution coordination. The blocks below summarize the main workstreams I contributed to and the practical validation gates used to turn lab work into deployment confidence.

Workstream
Cryogenic characterization

Optical module validation in cryogenic conditions

OE · FTS · electrical integrity · SQUID tuning · repeatable procedures

I performed cryogenic test campaigns to validate module-level performance and readiness: optical efficiency (OE) measurements, Fourier Transform Spectrometer (FTS) runs to verify spectral behavior, electrical checks/continuity screening, and SQUID tuning for stable readout operation. The emphasis was on clean procedures, repeatability, and fast troubleshooting to keep integration throughput high.

Validation gates (what “ready” means)
Electrical
Continuity / shorts screening + stable readout connectivity before cooldown
Optical
OE + FTS verification against expected band behavior (repeatable across runs)
Readout
SQUID tuning convergence + stable operating points suitable for instrument use
Workstream
Integration & micro-wirebonding

Module interconnects — micro-wirebonding leadership

Process discipline · inspection · coordination (Caltech × NASA JPL)

I led micro-wirebonding activities for optical module connections, coordinating with NASA JPL collaborators. The goal was to ensure clean routing, electrically robust bonds, and consistent workmanship appropriate for a South Pole deployment environment.

Integration gates
Inspection
Visual inspection + workmanship consistency checks prior to acceptance
Electrical
Continuity verification after bonding (and after any rework loop)
Handoff
Integration checkpoint: module declared mechanically clean + electrically robust
Workstream
Mechanical design

CMB-S4 prototype — wafer mechanical design responsibility

Interfaces · constraints · integration-minded CAD · manufacturability

I was responsible for mechanical design aspects of the wafer implementation for a Caltech-inspired CMB-S4 prototype. This included mechanical interfaces, constraints-driven geometry decisions, and integration-minded design choices to support testability and assembly.

Design gates
Interfaces
Interface definition + tolerance awareness to avoid integration surprises
Review-ready
CAD package suitable for design review and iteration with lab constraints
