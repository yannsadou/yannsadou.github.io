Mechanical design & integration of a dilution-fridge prototype for phonon-mediated MKID dark matter detectors

Over ~18 months at Caltech under Prof. Sunil Golwala, I contributed to the mechanical design, integration readiness, and hardware coordination of a prototype cryogenic payload supporting phonon-mediated kinetic inductance detector (KID/MKID) concepts for direct dark matter detection. The work emphasized practical deployability: tight clearances, connector access, thermal/mechanical interfaces, and repeatable assembly.

Context

What is this project (in one page)?

This effort sits in the R&D landscape of low-mass dark matter direct detection: phonons created by tiny energy deposits are sensed with superconducting resonators (MKIDs/KIDs) in a cryogenic environment. The design philosophy rewards low threshold, high multiplexing, and hardware repeatability—which makes the cryogenic payload and interfaces a first-order engineering problem.

  • Goal: push sensitivity with phonon-mediated superconducting sensor concepts (KIPM/KIPMD)
  • Hardware reality: tight clearances, connector access, cable routing, assembly discipline
  • Program context: cross-institution development paths aligned with low-background underground directions (e.g., SNOLAB)
My role

Fridge/payload mechanics as an “integration amplifier”

I worked on the mechanics that turn detector/RF concepts into a buildable cryogenic prototype: connector and coax interfaces, clearances (including tooling access), feedthrough placement, and CAD updates to keep the as-built configuration consistent with assembly reality.

  • Mechanical CAD iterations for cryogenic payload subassemblies and interfaces
  • Connector/access-driven design decisions (SMA/SMP constraints, wrench clearance)
  • Integration readiness: feedthrough placement, cable routing discipline, fit checks
  • Hardware tracking + design updates from real components and vendor models

Workstream
Fastener strategy

Washers / lock-washers / preload survival under cooldown

CTE-informed decisions · space constraints · CAD updates

A recurring reliability issue in cryogenic mechanics is “it assembled fine, then cooldown changed everything”. I analyzed where lock washers are needed vs. where they are not, and then pushed these decisions into the CAD model so the build stays consistent.

Design gates

Space reality
Where washers are needed, ensure there’s physical room to install them
