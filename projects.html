Hybrid Surface Power Architectures for Lunar & Martian Infrastructures

⟡
Heritage-driven benchmarking using lessons learned from ISS power distribution, Orion development flows, and the evolving Artemis surface architecture.

This project focuses on digital-twin-driven design of hybrid surface power systems for large-scale human-class infrastructures. The objective is to turn early architecture decisions into model-backed trade-offs that remain traceable as the system scales.

  • Digital twin–driven design of hybrid power systems for large surface infrastructures.
  • Integration of microgrids, PMAD networks, advanced storage, and ISRU-based generation.
  • Robustness, scalability, autonomy, reliability and mission-level performance under extreme constraints.

Cryogenic Detector Modules & Belleville Washer Stacks — BICEP Array

Engineering work supporting a large-scale scientific instrument operating at the South Pole, where thermal margins, interfaces, and integration repeatability matter. The work emphasized verification-oriented engineering: controlled preload strategies, interface design, module readiness, and test campaign support.

  • Design and analysis of mechanical stacks to maintain controlled preload at cryogenic temperatures.
  • Interface design between detector wafers, modules, and cryostat structures.
  • Support to integration and testing of CMB detector hardware.

Dark Matter Detector Instrumentation

Work on cryogenic instrumentation emphasized robust thermal architectures, mechanical stability, and verification-oriented design in vacuum environments. The mindset translates directly to space hardware: controlling interfaces, noise drivers, thermal paths, and integration sequences.

  • Mechanical and thermal optimization of cryostat and fridge design for phonon-mediated detectors.
  • Support to MKID-based instruments and related optics structures.
