CALTECH NASA JPL collaboration Hands-on lab build Zemax + SolidWorks NEW-MUSIC / MKIDs

From optical modeling to a working alignment rig for MKID antenna & sub-antenna mapping

During my end-of-study internship (ISAE-SUPAERO), I joined Prof. Sunil Golwala’s team at Caltech to support the development of Microwave Kinetic Inductance Detectors (MKIDs) for sub-millimeter astronomy. My core deliverable was an optical reconstruction setup that makes it possible to identify and map MKID antennas and sub-antennas outside the cryostat, by combining simulation (Zemax), mechanical design (SolidWorks), procurement, assembly, and measurement campaigns on the lab beam-mapper.

Context

Why this mattered

In the NEW-MUSIC instrument development flow, a critical step is being able to associate readout resonance features to the physical location of pixels (antennas / sub-antennas) in the detector array. This requires a reliable way to reconstruct the detector “image” on an external plane and compare it to the expected geometry.

  • Cryogenic detectors inside a cryostat (fixed geometry)
  • Beam-mapper used to scan a plane with a controlled source
  • Optical reconstruction needed to map antennas & resolve sub-antennas
  • Delivered as a robust lab setup, not a one-off demo

(Technical foundations: NEW-MUSIC context, cryostat + detectors, beam-mapper and mapping objective are documented in the internship report.)

My role

Ownership from modeling → hardware → measurements

I worked with strong autonomy (weekly team checkpoint) and carried the project end-to-end: optical design decisions, CAD, procurement, assembly integration, then measurement runs and iterative refinements.

  • Zemax model of the ellipsoidal mirror + fold mirror optical path
  • Mechanical design in SolidWorks using 8020 / standard frames and custom interfaces
  • Procurement & manufacturing coordination (McMaster-Carr, 8020, Xometry, shop support)
  • Assembly, alignment, measurement campaigns and repeatability improvements

Work packages

What I built

  • Optical model (Zemax): full path implementation, clearance trade studies, focus/plane selection
  • Mechanical system (SolidWorks): two rigid assemblies + coupling interfaces for repeatable alignment
  • Procurement & manufacturing: vendor coordination + custom parts + integration strategy
  • Lab integration: build, fit checks, alignment workflow, safe handling, iteration loops
  • Measurement campaign: beam-mapper scans, reconstruction, identification of antennas & sub-antennas
  • Repeatability engineering: guidance frames and structure modifications to reduce setup drift
Technical highlights

What was hard (and fun)

  • Bridging CAD geometry and Zemax parameterization (mirror representation + practical tolerances)
  • Managing obstruction/clearance constraints while preserving image reconstruction performance
  • Designing for repeatability: real lab setups move; your structure must “snap back” reliably
  • Balancing rigidity, mass, and adjustability (especially around the fold mirror)
  • Turning measurement plots into actionable mechanical changes

This project was a true Caltech × NASA JPL style workflow: simulation → hardware → test → iterate → document.

Results

Validated optical reconstruction

The final setup successfully reconstructed the detector image on the beam-mapper plane, enabling antenna identification and revealing sub-antenna structure in the maxima maps. Repeatability was improved through mechanical guidance features and more rigid coupling between assemblies.

  • Antenna pixels reconstructed with clear localization
  • Sub-antenna pattern visible as multiple maxima within antenna regions
  • Repeatability improved after structural refinements
