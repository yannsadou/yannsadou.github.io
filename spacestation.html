Feasibility study of a European commercial space station in Low-Earth Orbit

SEEDS XV is an intensive, international, hands-on program in which students rotate across multiple European institutions to carry out real systems-engineering studies. Our team developed a complete feasibility study of a commercial space station in LEO, covering mission context, space business development, high-level subsystem sizing, and scenario-driven reasoning for commercial space station operations. The project culminated in a final presentation at ESA's ESTEC and a final paper presented at the International Astronautical Congress 2023 (IAC) in Baku.

Context

What is SEEDS XV?

SEEDS XV is a European space-systems program structured as a multi-site project sprint. Participants work in interdisciplinary teams across several partner institutions such as ISAE-SUPAERO (France), University of Leicester (UK), and Politecnico di Torino (Italy), using systems engineering methods and mission design trade-offs to produce a cohesive study that is both technically grounded and clearly communicated.

  • Fast-paced systems engineering with real deliverables
  • Mission architecture + subsystem-level reasoning
  • International teamwork and iterative design reviews
  • Final dissemination through a conference paper
Project scope

Commercial Space Station in LEO feasibility

The objective of this study was to assess the feasibility of a European-led commercial space station in Low Earth Orbit, focusing on the concept of operations, market rationale, and industrial/business architecture rather than detailed subsystem engineering. We explored how a station could create value through science services, in-orbit manufacturing, technology demonstration, institutional partnerships, and commercial activities such as private missions and space tourism, while identifying the main programmatic drivers, constraints, and risks that shape the concept.

  • Value proposition and target users (institutional + commercial)
  • Use cases: research services, in-space manufacturing, tech demo, private astronaut missions & tourism
  • Concept of operations and utilization model (how the station is “used” day-to-day)
  • High-level architecture options and partnerships (transport, logistics, providers)
  • Programmatic constraints: schedule, cost drivers, regulatory/safety considerations (high level)
  • Risk-aware roadmap and development strategy for a European commercial approach

Work content

Key contributions (feasibility level)

  • Defined the commercial rationale: target users, services, and value proposition for a European LEO station
  • Mapped representative use-cases: research services, in-orbit manufacturing, tech demo, private missions & tourism
  • Built a concept of operations (how the station is utilized, supplied, crewed, and operated over time)
  • Explored high-level architecture options and partnership/logistics assumptions (transport, visiting vehicles, providers)
  • Identified major programmatic drivers (cost/schedule risks, safety & certification constraints, policy considerations) at high level
  • Iterated through reviews and incorporated feedback from ASI and the multi-site rotation
  • Delivered a coherent written feasibility study and a final conference-oriented publication package
Deliverables
  • Concept visuals (station configuration, modules, utilization scenarios)
  • Market / user segmentation snapshots and service “menu”
  • High-level roadmap, partnership landscape, and development strategy
  • Trade-off tables (concept options, utilization vs. complexity, ops choices)
  • Paper PDF / journal link and presentation deck links
